Brand guidelines

Logo & brand mark

How the RepresentDC mark is built, what it means, and how to use it consistently across the site, print, and social — plus FAQ-ready language explaining the design to visitors.

The mark

A five-pointed star with one point rendered differently from the other four.

ConstructionA regular 5-point star (outer radius 22, inner radius 9 on a 64×64 grid). The upper-left point carries a diagonal hatch texture instead of the flat navy fill used on the other four points.
Why a hatch, not just colorTexture — not hue alone — carries the meaning, so the mark reads identically to colorblind viewers and in single-color print or grayscale. Color (red, or gold on dark backgrounds) is layered on top for everyone else.
At small sizesBelow ~24px the hatch simplifies to a flat accent-colored wedge automatically — still a five-point star with one point set apart, just without the fine line detail.
Not the D.C. flagDeliberately not a recreation of the District's official three-stars-and-two-bars flag — that mark is used by nearly every statehood organization already. This is an original mark that references the same civic vocabulary (a star, a flag-red accent) without duplicating it.

Meaning & intent

Written so it can be dropped directly into the site's Myths & FAQ page.

BRANDWhy does the RepresentDC logo look the way it does?

The mark is a five-pointed star with one point set apart in texture and color. It represents D.C.'s incomplete standing in American democracy — one piece of full representation still missing, still being worked on, rather than a finished, static symbol.

That "different point" is built from a diagonal hatch pattern, not just a different color, so the idea comes through for colorblind visitors and in black-and-white print alike — a small design choice that mirrors the larger argument: equal representation shouldn't depend on who's looking or how.

We chose an original mark instead of the District's well-known three-stars-and-two-bars flag on purpose. That flag is a strong, recognizable symbol — and it's already the logo of nearly every D.C. statehood group. We wanted RepresentDC to be identifiable at a glance among them, while still speaking the same visual language: a star, a flag-red accent, a civic tone.

Wordmark

"RepresentDC" is always set as one word — never "Represent DC" with a space in the logo itself.

RepresentDC
TypefacePublic Sans, weight 800, letter-spacing -0.03em — the system's UI/heading face, kept at full brand weight for presence at small sizes.
"DC" treatmentSame weight and size as "Represent" — only the color changes (flag red on light backgrounds, gold on navy) with a thin underline matching the star's accent color. No boxed badge: a box read as two separate words, which fights the one-word brand name.
NeverDon't italicize, don't add a space ("Represent DC"), don't set "DC" in a different typeface, don't drop the underline tie to the mark.

Color & contrast

Three sanctioned combinations. Never introduce a fourth without a reason.

Full color, on white / paper

Default — web, most print.

#14213D#C8102E

Reversed, on navy

Footer, hero, dark UI. Gold replaces red so it never competes with the urgent-alert red used elsewhere on navy.

#fff#E0A500

Single-ink, monochrome

Letterhead, faxes, one-color print runs. Hatch becomes knockout white lines — still one ink.

#14213D only

Clear space & minimum size

Keep other elements out of the mark's breathing room, and don't shrink it past legibility.

Clear spaceMinimum margin on all sides equal to the width of one star point (roughly ¼ of the mark's total width) — the dashed box shown is that minimum.
Minimum digital size16px as a standalone favicon tile; 20px mark height when paired with the wordmark.
Minimum print size0.25in / 18pt mark height on letterhead and business cards.
Favicon tileRounded square, corner radius ≈23% of the tile's width, navy fill, mark centered and inset ~14%.

Don't

Common ways the mark gets diluted.

✕

Fill every point solid — loses the accessible "one point differs" signal entirely.

✕

Rotate, skew, or distort the proportions of the star.

✕

Add drop shadows, bevels, or gradients to the mark itself.
