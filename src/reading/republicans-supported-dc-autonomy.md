During what already is a contentious presidential election year, Americans can and should unite to support local autonomy for Washington, D.C., residents. [The United States](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/no-taxation-without-representation) was established after colonists forced to pay taxes but were denied representation in the British Parliament. American patriots fought in the Revolutionary War in large part to end [taxation without representation,](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/tax_without_representation.asp) their slogan against Britain.

However, centuries later, the U.S. still treats residents of our nation’s Capital in this disgraceful way. [Using a phrase](https://boundarystones.weta.org/2020/02/12/washington-taxation-without-representation-history) that is familiar with many Americans due to the Revolutionary War, D.C. [“Taxation Without Representation”](https://boundarystones.weta.org/2020/02/12/washington-taxation-without-representation-history) or [“End Taxation Without Representation”](https://dmv.dc.gov/service/end-taxation-without-representation-tags) are on some D.C. license plates. [D.C.’s residents](https://www.wusa9.com/article/news/verify/verify-yes-dc-residents-pay-the-highest-amount-in-federal-taxes-per-capita-heres-why-statehood-eleanor-holmes-norton-mayor-muriel-bowser-seante/65-ed274b53-2047-4a5a-9be7-b7b022f505d9#:~:text=Here%20are%20the%20five%20states%20paying%20the%20highest,4%20New%20York%3A%20%247%2C532%205%20New%20Jersey%3A%20%247%2C200) pay the highest federal taxes per capita and fight in our nation’s wars, but they have only [one](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/members/DC) non-voting delegate in the U.S. House of Representatives and no representation at all in the Senate. Since Congress must approve most legislation passed by the D.C. City Council, D.C. residents do not have control over the City’s local issues or any say in regional, national, and international issues. Let’s rectify this situation before the U.S. turns 250 years old in just a few short years.

[For long,](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/wjdt_665385/2649_665393/202303/t20230320_11044481.html#:~:text=According%20to%20a%20Pew%20Center,longer%20a%20model%20of%20democracy.) the U.S. had considered itself to be a model regarding democracy. However, according to a recent Pew Center poll’s result, 65 percent of Americans think that the American democracy needs major reforms and 57 percent of survey respondents think our nation no longer is a model of democracy. The U.S. is [the world’s only nation](https://www.commoncause.org/colorado/democracy-wire/dc-statehood-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/) with a representative democratic government that does not give its capital city’s residents any voting representation in its national legislature. We need to finally fix this grave injustice and make America first, not last, in democracies.

Unfortunately, Congressional voting representation and D.C.’s bid for statehood have become very politicized. However, D.C. local rights are actually a civil right, not a partisan, issue, and Republicans understood this point decades ago. As [former Congressman Tom Coleman](https://www.defendamericandemocracy.org/news/press-release-5-455zk) [(R-MO)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5db9d9e198db5517dd0e9eb4/t/60ca960afb70df3f190dfc52/1623889418620/210608-Conservative-Voices+on-Statehood.pdf) wrote in 2021 in his compelling [“DC REPRESENTATION USED](https://tomcoleman.net/2021/05/10/dc-representation-used-to-be-bipartisan-i-was-house-republican-voted-for-it/) TO BE A BIPARTISAN CAUSE. I WAS A HOUSE REPUBLICAN WHO VOTED FOR IT,” “the lack of Republican votes marked the distance the party has traveled over those four decades: from a willingness to consider a question of fundamental fairness on the merits, to no consideration whatsoever.”

This article focuses on Republicans because generally, Democrats support and Republicans oppose D.C. autonomy-related issues. It tries to show that Republicans supported D.C. in years past and thus now again.

## Background

Throughout history, there have been multiple efforts at granting D.C. residents at least some Congressional representation, local self-governance, and votes in the electoral college. Over time, the preferred choice for D.C. voting rights activists changed; their current top method is statehood. This section focuses on Congressional representation. Methods of achieving just Congressional representation have come in the following forms: full voting representation in the Senate and the House (via a [bill](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-109hr5410ih) or a [Constitutional amendment](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/dc-statehood-explained)), [Utah/D.C.](https://www.deseret.com/2007/2/16/20002430/house-may-soon-vote-on-seats-for-utah-d-c/) compromise in the late 2000s (that would have granted D.C. and Utah a vote each in the House but would not have changed the Senate), and [semi-retrocession](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33830.html) ([D.C. residents](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-108hhrg96625/html/CHRG-108hhrg96625.htm) vote in Maryland’s elections – [This options harkens back](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33830.html#_Toc345851835) to between 1790 and 1800, when D.C. residents could vote in the elections of Maryland and Virginia).

[Voting representation](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/549833-gop-senator-introduces-bill-to-make-dc-part-of-maryland/) could also be achieved through D.C. becoming its [own state](https://sgp.fas.org/crs/misc/IF11443.pdf) or [D.C. joining Maryland](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/549833-gop-senator-introduces-bill-to-make-dc-part-of-maryland/). If D.C. became its own state (as [37 states](https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/pdf/R/R47101) [of](https://norton.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/norton-responds-to-incorrect-assertion-that-23rd-amendment-must-be) the nation’s 50 states have done since the U.S. became a country) or joined another state, D.C. would have more power than if it received just Congressional voting representation. In recent years, some Republicans have favored retrocession. [As Senator Roger Marshall (R-KS)](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/549833-gop-senator-introduces-bill-to-make-dc-part-of-maryland/) said in 2021, “If the Democrats want D.C. statehood, make it part of Maryland.” [In 2021,](https://www.cnn.com/2021/05/03/politics/dc-statehood-susan-collins-maryland/index.html) Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) said, [“There is a way](https://www.cnn.com/2021/05/03/politics/dc-statehood-susan-collins-maryland/index.html) to ensure that the residents of DC have voting representation in Congress, and that is for DC to become part of Maryland, just as parts of DC became parts of Virginia many years ago,” However, [many oppose](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/05/dc-statehood-retrocession-maryland/618788/) retrocession of much of D.C.’s land back to Maryland, which along with Virginia gave land to create D.C.

Local D.C. rights had been bipartisan. In addition, sometimes, Republicans led the way on D.C. local autonomy. [In 1877,](https://books.google.com/books?id=MaQTAAAAIAAJ&pg=PA37&lpg=PA37&dq=%E2%80%9CIn+the+District+of+Columbia+the+time+is+long+overdue+for+granting+national+suffrage+to+its+citizens+and+also+applying+the+principle+of+local+self-government+to+the+Nation%E2%80%99s+Capital.%E2%80%9D+eisenhower&source=bl&ots=x4n7BhmC8g&sig=ACfU3U2FL9j_l1aXnj_r58HZMsWcAQwFsA&hl=en&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwiatMK_rIWFAxULMVkFHVeUBTwQ6AF6BAgIEAM#v=onepage&q=%E2%80%9CIn%20the%20District%20of%20Columbia%20the%20time%20is%20long%20overdue%20for%20granting%20national%20suffrage%20to%20its%20citizens%20and%20also%20applying%20the%20principle%20of%20local%20self-government%20to%20the%20Nation%E2%80%99s%20Capital.%E2%80%9D%20eisenhower&f=false) [Delegate](https://bioguide.congress.gov/search/bio/C000777) William Corlett (R-WY) [introduced the first resolution](https://books.google.com/books?id=MaQTAAAAIAAJ&pg=PA37&lpg=PA37&dq=%E2%80%9CIn+the+District+of+Columbia+the+time+is+long+overdue+for+granting+national+suffrage+to+its+citizens+and+also+applying+the+principle+of+local+self-government+to+the+Nation%E2%80%99s+Capital.%E2%80%9D+eisenhower&source=bl&ots=x4n7BhmC8g&sig=ACfU3U2FL9j_l1aXnj_r58HZMsWcAQwFsA&hl=en&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwiatMK_rIWFAxULMVkFHVeUBTwQ6AF6BAgIEAM#v=onepage&q=%E2%80%9CIn%20the%20District%20of%20Columbia%20the%20time%20is%20long%20overdue%20for%20granting%20national%20suffrage%20to%20its%20citizens%20and%20also%20applying%20the%20principle%20of%20local%20self-government%20to%20the%20Nation%E2%80%99s%20Capital.%E2%80%9D%20eisenhower&f=false) to give national representation to D.C. [On April 3, 1888,](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33830.html)  [Senator](https://bioguide.congress.gov/search/bio/B000524) Henry Blair (R-NH) [introduced](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1978/08/23/dc-chasing-full-voting-rights-since-1801/ffa23567-0ec6-433e-8e22-767c6480720d/) the first Constitutional amendment that would have granted Congressional representation to D.C. residents. The Republican party was extremely different during the late 1800’s, of course, but [Republicans](https://www.npr.org/2019/07/20/743650584/opinion-should-republicans-still-call-themselves-the-party-of-lincoln) today still pride themselves as the party of President Abraham Lincoln.

## Previous Bipartisan Support for Congressional Representation for D.C.

In the past (mainly decades ago but also in the late 2000’s to a limited extent), Democrats and Republicans united in their support for D.C. voting rights. Both Democrats’ and Republicans’ party platforms contained strong support for Washington, D.C. The following statements come from Republican platforms over the past sixty years:

- [1960](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) – [“Republicans will](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) [continue](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/republican-party-platform-1960) to work for Congressional representation and self-government for the District of Columbia and also support the constitutional amendment \[(the 23rd Amendment of 1961)\] granting suffrage in national elections.”

- 1968 – [“we specifically](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) favor representation in Congress for the District of Columbia”

- 1972 – [“We support voting](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) representation for the District of Columbia in the United States Congress and will work for a system of self-government for the city which takes fair account of the needs and interests of both the Federal Government and the citizens of the District of Columbia.”

- 1976 – [“We again](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/)...support giving the District of Columbia voting representation in the United States Senate and House of Representatives and full home rule over those matters that are purely local.”

Decades ago, Democrats and Republicans were instrumental in granting D.C. home rule [(DCNOW’s Trying](https://www.dc-now.org/post/trying-to-end-home-rule) to End Home Rule blog post discusses this topic) and votes in the electoral college. [In the early 1970s](https://www.dc-now.org/post/trying-to-end-home-rule), Congress passed the District Home Rule Act, which granted limited home rule to D.C. residents. Republican President Richard Nixon signed the bill into law. When [signing](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-signing-the-district-columbia-self-government-and-governmental-reorganization) this monumental legislation, he said

[As a longtime supporter](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-signing-the-district-columbia-self-government-and-governmental-reorganization) of self-government for the District of Columbia, I am pleased to sign into law a measure which is of historic significance for the citizens of our Nation's Capital. I first voted for home rule as a Member of the House of Representatives in 1948, and I have endorsed the enactment of home rule legislation during both my terms as President.

[The 23rd Amendment to the U.S. Constitution](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/constitutional-amendments-amendment-23-extending-vote-district-columbia#:~:text=Amendment%20Twenty%2Dthree%20to%20the,may%20participate%20in%20presidential%20elections.) granted votes in the electoral college to Washington, D.C. Under this amendment, D.C. would get the same number of electoral college votes as the least-populous state. The amendment states that via legislation, Congress must have the authority to enforce it.

The [23rd amendment](https://time.com/4267840/23rd-amendment/) was adopted during the early-1960’s civil rights movement [(an effort](https://www.google.com/search?q=civl+rights+and+black&rlz=1C1RXQR_enUS1013US1013&oq=civl+rights+and+black&gs_lcrp=EgZjaHJvbWUyBggAEEUYOTIGCAEQRRg8MgkIAhAAGA0YgAQyCQgDEAAYDRiABDIJCAQQABgNGIAEMggIBRAAGBYYHjIICAYQABgWGB4yCAgHEAAYFhge0gEIMjA4MmowajeoAgCwAgA&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8) for equality for African-Americans in the U.S.) [when African-Americans](https://time.com/4267840/23rd-amendment/) comprised the majority of D.C. residents. [Senator Kenneth Keating](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/06/republicans-used-back-dc-statehood-what-changed/619221/) (R-NY) introduced the amendment in 1960 and led Congressional efforts for its passage. [Passed within months by Congress](https://time.com/4267840/23rd-amendment/)  during the summer of 1960, this amendment became the second-most quickly ratified Constitutional amendment although only some states ratified this amendment. [Many Southern](https://www.nytimes.com/2011/03/29/opinion/29masur.html) states did not ratify the Amendment. In fact, Tennessee was the only former Confederate state to ratify this bill. However, multiple Republican-led state legislatures in the North ratified this amendment, proving that racial politics were more crucial than partisanship in affecting ratification. Republican President Dwight Eisenhower supported this amendment. [On March 29, 1961,](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/constitutional-amendments-amendment-23-extending-vote-district-columbia) the Amendment was ratified, and D.C. gained three votes in the electoral college.

Efforts to acquire Congressional voting representation via a Constitutional amendment have, so far, not been successful. [In 1978, Senator Edward Brooke (R-MA)](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/06/republicans-used-back-dc-statehood-what-changed/619221/) introduced the U.S. Senate measure, and [both the House](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) and the Senate passed a joint resolution by the requisite two-thirds majority. In the House, while 226 Democrats (77 percent) and 63 Republicans (44 percent) voted for this resolution, 48 Democrats (16 percent) and 79 Republicans (55 percent) voted against it. [In the U.S. Senate,](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1978/08/23/senate-roll-call-on-dc-voting-bill/a84ef622-c4eb-4e3c-9342-de374017333b/) [while 48 Democrats](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) (79 percent) and 19 Republicans (50 percent) voted for this resolution, 12 Democrats (20 percent) and 19 Republicans (50 percent) voted against it. [If just one more](https://tomcoleman.net/2021/05/10/dc-representation-used-to-be-bipartisan-i-was-house-republican-voted-for-it/) Republican Senator had voted against the resolution, that resolution would have not passed. [Republican National](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Research/Publications/Bkground/BP79-03.pdf) Chairman [Bill](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1978/09/28/dc-ratification-suffers-setback-in-pennsylvania/c764e7af-f19c-4522-b82c-a27258658ffd/) [Brock](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Research/Publications/Bkground/BP79-03.pdf) supported that amendment. [Only 16 states](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) ratified this potential amendment before the [deadline](http://www.dcstatehoodcoalition.org/a-partisan-history-of-reform/) expired in 1985. Hence, this amendment fell far short at the state level. [It had needed 38 states](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/dc-statehood-explained) for ratification.

## Republicans’ Current Anti-D.C. Views Contradict Their Long-Held Support of State/Local Rights

The current Congress’ (2023-2024) interference in local D.C. affairs, primarily by  Republicans, contradicts [Republicans’](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Republican-Party/Policy-and-structure) traditional support of states’/local rights and sometimes the federal regulation of typical state and local issues (including policing and education). [Congress](https://statehood.dc.gov/page/congressional-intervention#:~:text=Under%20the%20District%20Clause%20of,or%20even%20overturn%20such%20legislation.) has jurisdiction over the nation’s capital and thus can oversee D.C. However, the current Congress has gone overboard in disrespecting D.C.’s self-governance (especially on traditional local issues, including policing and elections). It prevented D.C.’s crime bill from becoming law in 2023 [(DCNOW’s blog’s “Fears-Turned Reality:](https://www.dc-now.org/post/fears-turned-reality-congress-and-the-district-of-columbia-in-2023-so-far) Congress and the District of Columbia in 2023 So Far” post mentions this major meddling.), and it held multiple hearings relating to crime in D.C. Congressional interference has gotten worse, with some Republicans wanting to dictate how D.C. handles some crime-related matters and to use D.C. as a model for their election laws.       

## Partisan Make-Up Should Not/Does Not Make a Difference Regarding Congressional Representation

The fact that DC is a majority Democratic city should not affect its citizens’ right to vote. The [50 states’ political party](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/religious-landscape-study/compare/party-affiliation/by/state/) affiliations vary tremendously, but all states have full Congressional voting representation.

No one says that a certain [state](https://www.weforum.org/agenda/2020/11/animated-map-u-s-presidential-voting-history-by-state-1976-2016/) (i.e. – very Republican Wyoming or extremely Democratic Massachusetts) should not be represented in Congress due to their citizens’ political party affiliation. Thus, why is D.C.’s mainly Democratic composition such a barrier to voting rights? If D.C. were mainly Republican, its residents still would deserve to be treated the same as other Americans are treated.

## D.C. Would Not Necessarily Elect Just Democrats to Congress

Republicans’ main opposition to D.C. statehood is that statehood is power grab by Democrats. [As U.S. Representative](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andrewsolender/2021/04/21/these-are-the-wildest-republican-arguments-against-dc-statehood/?sh=6033f3cf5bf7) Steve Scalise (R-LA) wrote about a D.C. statehood bill during the past few years, [“This legislation](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andrewsolender/2021/04/21/these-are-the-wildest-republican-arguments-against-dc-statehood/?sh=6033f3cf5bf7) violates the 23rd Amendment and is nothing more than a partisan power grab from Speaker Pelosi and her socialist allies to add two more Democrats to the Senate.” In addition, [Representative Chris](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2021/4/21/22395777/dc-statehood-about-fair-representation-democratic-power-grab-romney-biden-lee-stewart/) Stewart (R-UT) said in 2021, [“This is about politics.](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2021/4/21/22395777/dc-statehood-about-fair-representation-democratic-power-grab-romney-biden-lee-stewart/) This is about getting two solid Democratic members of the Senate. That allows them to pack the Supreme Court, allows them to break the filibuster, allows the Green New Deal, allows a whole bunch of incredibly progressive even radical legislation, and that’s what they’re trying to achieve by this.”

However, if Washington, D.C., is granted voting representation in Congress, [it might not](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/everyone-knows-why-republicans-really-150216154.html) necessarily elect only Democrats. States’ residents do not always vote as expected. Alaska joined the U.S. as a Democratic state, and Hawaii joined as a Republican state. However, over the past 50 years, those two states’ citizens’ political affiliations reversed:  Alaska is now mainly Republican and Hawaii, largely Democratic.

The only entities to vote Democratic that year were Massachusetts and D.C. ## A Plurality of Americans Favor D.C. Statehood

Although [over time, Americans’](https://news.gallup.com/poll/260129/americans-reject-statehood.aspx) support for D.C. statehood has fluctuated, as of 2021, a plurality of Americans support D.C. statehood. See the below table and graph:

| [1989 Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/260129/americans-reject-statehood.aspx) | 31% | 52% | 17% |
| [1992 Yankelovich/Clancy/Shulman](https://news.gallup.com/poll/260129/americans-reject-statehood.aspx) | 20% | 57% | 24% |

[In 2019,](https://www.filesforprogress.org/datasets/2021/3/DFA-DFP-DC-Statehood.pdf) most people in swing states supported D.C. statehood, making it a possible campaign issue. In those states, 57 percent supported, 33 percent opposed, and ten percent did not have an opinion on this topic.

- Voters throughout the U.S. supported D.C. statehood.

- [The argument](https://www.filesforprogress.org/datasets/2021/3/DFA-DFP-DC-Statehood.pdf) that tends to get support for D.C. statehood among voters from all over the U.S., across the political spectrum, and with different backgrounds was [“taxation](https://www.filesforprogress.org/datasets/2021/3/DFA-DFP-DC-Statehood.pdf) without representation.” This point connected with 73 percent of Democrats, 55 percent of Independents, and 42 percent (the largest segment of support) of Republicans.

- Most voters support D.C. statehood even when people see the critics’ [“power grab”](https://www.filesforprogress.org/datasets/2021/3/DFA-DFP-DC-Statehood.pdf) argument. That argument is the Republicans’ main argument against statehood.

- [The racial justice element](https://www.filesforprogress.org/datasets/2021/3/DFA-DFP-DC-Statehood.pdf) is salient for Democrats, but not Republicans (although D.C. seemed to have gained electoral college votes at least in part due to concerns about racial justice). While 78 percent of Democrats support D.C. statehood when connected with race, Republicans are the most opposed when this tie is made. [According to an unnamed retired moderate House Republican,](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/everyone-knows-why-republicans-really-150216154.html) race has long been a major reason why Republicans oppose statehood. As that former Member of Congress said, [“This is outrageous](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/everyone-knows-why-republicans-really-150216154.html) — my fellow party members are opposing it simply because the representatives would be Black and Democratic. If this were a white city… they would all be for it because of the taxation without representation.”

## Call to Action

Let’s come together as a nation and support autonomy for D.C. residents. Congress needs to end its continued interference in local D.C. affairs and to grant D.C. statehood. If D.C. were a state, Congress would not be able to continue its current meddling. We can learn from lessons of the 23rd Amendment. For the good of the nation and basic human and civil rights, Republicans should set aside partisan opposition and grant statehood for the District of Columbia, ensuring fairness and justice for D.C. residents.
